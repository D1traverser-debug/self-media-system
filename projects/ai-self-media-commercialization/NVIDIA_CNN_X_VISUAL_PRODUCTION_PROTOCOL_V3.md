## 2. Source ledger and factual boundaries

Read the article and current authoritative updates before committing to a visual claim. The article is not the only source of current status: a later official update can narrow or change what a new image may fairly imply.

### F01 — Megaspeed investigation, not a proven smuggling conviction

CNN reports concerns that Singapore-based Megaspeed may have diverted advanced Nvidia chips to entities in China. Megaspeed denied wrongdoing and described itself as compliant and legal. CNN also reports Nvidia said its China business went to zero after April 2025 US trade restrictions.

On November 17, 2026, the US Departments of Commerce and Justice stated that Commerce began investigating Megaspeed in 2024; requested records from Nvidia in May 2025; Nvidia put Megaspeed on its no-sell list in June 2025; Commerce and Singaporean law enforcement conducted an on-site end-use check at Megaspeed in June 2025 and interviewed its CEO; DOJ then took the lead in the criminal investigation. The statement refers to any criminal conduct that may have occurred; it does not announce a conviction.

Permitted visual treatment: export-control scrutiny, an investigation, a questioned route, or a suspected diversion mechanism.  
Not permitted: presenting Megaspeed, Alice Huang, or any named person as a proven smuggler, spy, or criminal.

### F02 — Singapore-to-China physical diversion route

The CNN report identifies Singapore-based Megaspeed and concerns about advanced chips reaching entities in China. The Washington Post separately describes suspected physical smuggling and regional intermediary/cloud-provider routes.

Permitted: a clearly marked hypothetical/suspected route between Singapore and China.  
Not permitted: presenting the illustrated route as a proven route used by Megaspeed, inventing a specific port, or implying that the Singapore Merlion or Chinese panda is an actual participant.

### F03 — Remote cloud access is distinct from physical chip movement

The Washington Post reports that some overseas intermediary companies buy Nvidia chips and rent remote access to them over the internet, potentially giving Chinese companies access to chips located abroad. This is a different mechanism from physically moving the chips into China.

Permitted: a generic remote-compute access loophole, clearly distinct from physical smuggling.  
Not permitted: claiming without evidence that Megaspeed specifically provided a particular remote-access service to a named Chinese customer.

### F04 — Interagency disagreement and the Entity List

Reuters reported Scott Bessent saying Treasury, the Pentagon, State Department and USTR favored adding Megaspeed to the Commerce Department Entity List, while Commerce controlled the decision. CNN reported public disagreement between Treasury and Commerce.

The November 17 DOJ/Commerce statement also documents Commerce's earlier investigation, record request and on-site end-use check. Therefore the illustration may satirize who controls the final pen and the reported interagency disagreement; it must not claim Commerce did nothing, was inactive throughout, or acted corruptly.

### F05 — Super Micro is a separate case

CNN reported that Super Micro's founder was charged in March in connection with an alleged server-smuggling scheme involving Nvidia chips. The Washington Post clarifies that three individuals were charged and Super Micro itself was not charged; the company said it was cooperating.

Permitted: distinguishing the separate Megaspeed investigation from charges against individuals in the Super Micro matter.  
Not permitted: depicting Super Micro itself as a convicted or charged corporate defendant, or transferring the Super Micro allegations to Megaspeed.

### F06 — AI infrastructure financing

CNN reports Nvidia joined major financial institutions in lending more than $500 billion for AI infrastructure. CNN separately reports Nvidia committed $105 billion toward a large OpenAI data-center project.

These are separate reported financing/investment facts. Do not combine the $500 billion lending and $105 billion commitment into one transaction or imply that every dollar follows one literal circular path. The visual may satirize the overlapping incentives between financing AI infrastructure and selling the hardware it buys.

### F07 — China market estimate

CNN reports Jensen Huang said at the 2025 Milken Institute annual meeting that the eventual China AI-chip market could reach $50 billion. This is a reported estimate, not realized revenue or a verified current market size.

Use the number only with an explicit “estimate” framing in post-production copy. Do not render it as actual Nvidia sales.

### F08 — Article's editorial thesis

CNN's central point is that sensational personal allegations risk obscuring the larger questions around chip exports, oversight, policy, and AI capital flows.

The satire should target systems, incentives, institutions, policy contradictions and financial structures. Do not use sexual allegations, private-life speculation or unverified intelligence claims as the punchline.

## 4. Art-direction research translated into production rules

The research does not justify copying a named artist. It supports transferable visual methods:

- Chinese manhua traditions combine economy of line with a strong underlying idea; caricature, satire, ink drawing and visual shorthand can carry social critique. Use this as a method reference, not as an imitation of a living artist.
- The National Archives' political-cartoon teaching material identifies analogy, caricature, captioning/labels, exaggeration, irony, juxtaposition and symbolism as distinct persuasion tools. Each concept must choose one primary mechanism rather than stacking all of them.
- The Metropolitan Museum's survey of caricature and satire describes a broad toolkit ranging from exaggerated portraits to fantastic forms made from inanimate objects and animals acting like humans. The selected mechanism must serve the editorial thesis.
- Research on visual metaphor stresses that an image must be interpreted in its context and through the audience's expected prior knowledge. If a viewer needs niche context, add one simple anchor or redesign the image.
- Composition research emphasizes salience through size, contrast, sharpness and foreground placement, plus framing through boundaries and negative space. Each image therefore gets one dominant focal relation.

## 5. CAOS-governed recursive workflow

CAOS governance applies to every substantive decision and action: source interpretation, concept choice, research, art direction, prompt writing, tool selection, generation, inspection, correction and acceptance. It is a recursive dependency graph, not a one-pass checklist. Any new evidence may reopen an earlier node.

### Node A — Recover the full goal and current evidence

- Read this task record, latest owner corrections, current prompt pack, source ledger and previous failure evidence.
- Treat prior images as rejected evidence, never as generation references.
- Resolve the exact task branch and file versions before editing.
- Keep the full owner outcome intact when the active working context is compressed.

### Node B — Build and challenge the fact model

- Separate established events, attributed reporting, allegations, denials, official statements, estimates and unknowns.
- Check later primary/official updates against the original article.
- For every proposed visual claim, record the source and the strongest counterevidence.
- If the fact cannot be stated fairly in one sentence, do not turn it into a visual accusation.

### Node C — Compete concepts before selecting one

For each slot, develop at least three materially different joke mechanisms. Score each against:
1. article specificity;
2. two-second first-read clarity;
3. originality;
4. visual executability;
5. fairness and factual precision;
6. separation from the other seven concepts;
7. thumbnail readability.

Reject a concept if it needs a paragraph to explain, depends on an unsupported allegation, repeats another image's thesis, or requires too many independent visual relationships.

### Node D — Freeze the visual sentence

Write one sentence in this form:

**[main subject] does [one visible action] to/with [one meaningful object], producing [one absurd but readable consequence].**

Then specify:
- what a viewer should infer at first glance;
- what knowledge is assumed;
- what one visual relation proves the joke;
- what the image must not imply.

If the visual sentence cannot be staged in one still frame, change the concept before writing the prompt.

### Node E — Research and choose the art grammar

Research only what changes the design decision. Inspect relevant examples and extract:
- line / shape / silhouette;
- composition and focal hierarchy;
- visual metaphor or caricature mechanism;
- print / material / texture behavior;
- how the style supports the joke.

Do not add style names as decoration. Each of the eight must have a distinct medium, palette and composition for a reason.

### Node F — Build a scene graph before prose

Specify the scene in a compact structured plan:
- camera/viewpoint and horizon;
- foreground, middle ground and background;
- exact people / animals / props counts;
- each object's location and orientation;
- contact, attachment, support, occlusion and gravity;
- movement direction and cause/effect;
- light direction and shadow consistency;
- text/no-text decision and safe placement.

Draw or describe a simple spatial layout before prompt compilation. Do not rely on vague phrases such as “nearby,” “around,” “behind” or “connected” when exact placement matters.

### Node G — Compile one prompt for one image

The prompt must be a self-contained execution specification, not a research essay. Use:
1. task and output format;
2. one-sentence visual thesis;
3. exact scene and camera;
4. object/character counts and spatial relations;
5. focal hierarchy and gesture/action;
6. concept-specific art direction;
7. lighting, palette and materials;
8. typography contract;
9. only the few exclusions needed to prevent known catastrophic errors.

Prefer positive observable instructions. Do not paste the whole article, a design Bible, generic style bundles, or long negative-prompt dumps into the generator.

### Node H — Prompt preflight

Before generation, challenge the exact prompt:
- Does the first sentence describe the intended joke, not merely the topic?
- Is there exactly one main action and one focal relationship?
- Can every noun be located in the scene?
- Are object and character counts explicit?
- Is the physical action possible and visually readable?
- Are route, map, arrows, cables and money-flow directions unambiguous?
- Are there any duplicated people, reflections, extra limbs or accidental extra props?
- Does the prompt accidentally turn an allegation into fact?
- Does the chosen style conflict with the composition or the X thumbnail use?
- Is the requested text actually necessary, and should it be added in post instead?

If any answer is unresolved, do not generate.

### Node I — Generate sequentially

- Generate exactly one image for the active concept.
- Do not generate the next image until the active one has a documented PASS, REWORK or REJECT decision.
- Bind the exact prompt version and generation call/result to the image.
- Do not silently change the prompt after generation; create a new prompt version for a new attempt.
- Do not feed rejected candidates back as references.

### Node J — Image-only cold read

Before consulting the concept brief again, write a literal description of what is visibly happening and infer the joke from the image alone. Record this as an assistant cold-read, not an independent human review. It must not be described as independently verified.

If the literal scene does not lead naturally to the intended joke, the image fails even if the assistant remembers the prompt.

### Node K — Direct visual craft QA

Inspect the actual rendered image, not the prompt. Check:
- two-second joke recognition;
- correct article-specific meaning;
- focal hierarchy and negative space;
- exact person / animal / prop counts;
- face, hands, fingers and limb anatomy;
- object geometry, contact, support and occlusion;
- map geography, route direction, cable direction and flow direction;
- perspective, scale and floor plane;
- lighting, shadows and reflections;
- legibility and spelling of any post-production text;
- style consistency and visual clutter;
- absence of defamatory or unsupported implications.

### Node L — Localize the earliest failure and reopen upstream when needed

Classify the failure as one or more of:
- source/fact interpretation;
- editorial thesis;
- concept/joke mechanism;
- art research / style selection;
- visual sentence;
- scene graph / spatial staging;
- prompt compilation;
- generation variance / model limitation;
- visual QA / acceptance.

Do not automatically blame the last prompt line or the image model. A prompt cannot repair an incoherent concept. If the selected diagnosis fails its counterexample, reopen the earliest affected dependency. Change one causal layer at a time where possible; redesign the whole concept when the concept itself is weak.

### Node M — Acceptance and learning

A PASS requires:
- a clear standalone joke;
- source-faithful meaning;
- coherent physical staging;
- clean anatomy and object logic;
- a distinct style and concept;
- no reliance on a long caption;
- an exact prompt/result record;
- an explicit QA receipt.

A REWORK image is not accepted. A visually attractive but semantically unclear image is not accepted. A prompt that looks detailed is not accepted. An assistant cold-read is not independent human validation.

After each image, record the failure mode, correction, what changed, and what reusable method is actually supported by the result. Then proceed to the next concept.

## 6. Per-image decision receipt

Every image must have a compact record containing:

- image ID and prompt version;
- source facts and links;
- thesis and joke mechanism;
- competing concepts and why the winner beat them;
- intended first-read;
- art references inspected and transferable principles;
- scene graph / exact object counts;
- typography decision and final overlay;
- factual / ethical boundary;
- exact generation prompt and call receipt;
- literal image-only cold read;
- visual QA findings;
- earliest failure layer, if any;
- revision and independent readback;
- PASS / REWORK / REJECT with evidence;
- unresolved unknowns.

Do not mark missing provenance as complete. Use UNKNOWN.

## 7. Global stop rules

Stop before generation if:
- the source claim is unresolved;
- the concept depends on an allegation treated as fact;
- the visual sentence requires multiple panels or a paragraph;
- the spatial plan contains ambiguous object relations;
- the prompt contains conflicting instructions;
- the selected concept duplicates another image.

Reject after generation if:
- the intended joke cannot be inferred from the image alone;
- the visual implies an unsupported crime or named-person allegation;
- the scene has a critical spatial, anatomy or object-count defect;
- the image needs a long caption to explain its meaning.

No publication or release authority is granted by this protocol. The eight-image task remains candidate production until all normal editorial, rights, review and release gates are met.
