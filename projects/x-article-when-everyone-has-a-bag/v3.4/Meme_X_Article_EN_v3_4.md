# When Everyone Has a Bag, Who Do You Believe?

*Everyone can make a meme famous. Not everyone gets paid when its token runs.*

Phillip Banks created the Chill Guy character. In November 2024, a Solana token built around it was surging, though its creator was unknown. Traders on Crypto Twitter asked Banks to post a Solana address so they could send him money or tokens.

Banks said the character was copyrighted and that he planned takedowns for unauthorized merchandise and shitcoins. Brand accounts using it as a trend were different; he mainly wanted credit. [CoinDesk reported the exchange.](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2024/11/21/chillguy-creator-threatens-legal-action-as-crypto-trenches-scour-tiktok)

The episode put three roles around one meme: the artist who created the character, the unknown person who launched the token, and traders who wanted the artist to receive money or tokens. They were all connected to Chill Guy, but each had a different relationship to its value.

The word **community** can fold those roles together. It may refer to the people who make a meme travel, the wallets that own its token, or the accounts that receive money when it trades. Those groups can overlap, but their contributions and rewards still need to be identified separately.

## 01 / A meme can travel. Its token has owners.

Memes move because people can reuse them. They redraw the image, change the caption, splice it into a new joke and carry it into another audience. A Bigfoot joke can travel across X in a thousand remixes; the token attached to it still has its own holder list.

A ticker adds an ownership layer to the same cultural object: a supply, a market price and wallets that hold or sell it. The people sharing the joke may have little overlap with the people holding the token.

Virality shows how far the culture has travelled. It says little about how widely the token is held.

## 02 / The chart starts advertising the meme.

When a post catches, price can become part of the content. Buyers push the price up. A holder screenshots the green candle and posts it back into the feed. The screenshot draws new eyes to the meme; some viewers become buyers. Their trades can produce the next screenshot.

A November 2026 study by Tomas Jais and Reilly White finds stronger spillovers and attention-driven trading among community tokens. It documents a broader market pattern rather than tracing the cause of any single rally. [Read the study.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0275531926003491)

Once the chart is circulating as content, it is doing more than recording a price. It is helping distribute the story.

## 03 / You can be early to the post and late to the launch.

Bitquery examined 8,212,568 Pump.fun launches whose first trades occurred between October 30, 2025 and October 7, 2026. Under its on-chain rules, developer-linked wallets bought in the launch block on 21% of launches. On 65%, the developer's first sale came within one minute. [Read Bitquery's investigation.](https://www.bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)

Bitquery links developer wallets through the launch transaction, fee payment and the four transactions immediately after it. These are on-chain classifications, not verified identities; a first sale does not show that the entire position was sold. The figures describe wallet patterns and timing, not a scam rate.

By the time a token reaches your X feed, some of its earliest positions may already be in place. Your discovery time and the token's launch timeline run on different clocks.

Market cap multiplies the quoted price by supply. It does not tell you how much money entered or how much liquidity is available for an exit.

## 04 / The person posting may also own the trade.

Fan art, translations, moderation, spaces, raids and daily replies keep a meme in circulation. A fan-art post introduces it to a new audience; a raid carries it into another timeline; moderation keeps newcomers around. For a holder, that work can express genuine attachment and help keep the token visible at the same time.

A holder's post carries both a view of the project and a position in its outcome. Check whether the account discloses that position, what it contributes beyond price calls, and what remains when trading slows. Original work and ongoing community labor tell you something different from a feed built around chart screenshots.

## 05 / "Community" is three different ledgers.

To see what the word community actually covers, separate three ledgers:

1. **Culture:** Who created the original meme? Who keeps it alive through edits, translations, events and moderation?
2. **Ownership:** Who received the supply, when did they get it, how concentrated are the holdings, and which wallets can sell into new attention?
3. **Cash flow:** Who receives creator fees, holder rewards or other payments when the token trades?

Pump.fun shows how the last ledger can be configured. Its documentation says a creator can choose at launch to direct the Creator fee to eligible holders instead of to the creator. Holder Rewards have eligibility rules and distribution costs; payments are not guaranteed. [Pump.fun's fee documentation.](https://pump.fun/docs/fees)

An early buyer supplies capital; an artist supplies the character; a moderator supplies labor. The fee settings determine which wallets receive the platform payment. One person can fill several roles, but the holder list and fee route show which roles the token's economics actually reward.

## 06 / Dogecoin once turned online enthusiasm into a fundraiser.

In 2014, the Dogecoin Foundation organized the Dogesled fundraiser, which raised more than $30,000 for Jamaica's two-man bobsled team to travel to the Sochi Winter Olympics. [PBS covered the fundraiser.](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/jamaican-bobsled-team-raises-30000-in-dogecoin-for-trip-to-sochi)

That is a community producing an outcome beyond a token's price: people rallied around an internet currency and helped a team get to the Games. The fundraiser is evidence of collective action. Ownership and fee distribution in a later token still need their own accounting.

## 07 / Match the claim to the evidence.

Before buying into a community pitch, match each claim to the evidence:

- A cultural claim calls for visible work: what people keep making, translating and organizing when the chart is quiet.
- An ownership claim calls for wallet data: who holds the supply and when early positions formed.
- A shared-upside claim calls for the fee route: who receives payments and whether promoters disclose their positions.

A busy timeline shows that people are active. The holder list and fee route show who owns the token and who gets paid when it trades. Community tells you who gathered around the meme; the wallets and fee rules show how the token's benefits are distributed.
