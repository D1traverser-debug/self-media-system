# When Everyone Has a Bag, Who Do You Believe?

*Everyone can make a meme famous. Not everyone gets paid when its token runs.*

During the 2024 Chill Guy frenzy, one Crypto Twitter trader had a suggestion for the character's creator: ask for 2% of the token supply. “As is tradition,” he joked.

The percentage was a joke; the instinct behind it was serious. Phillip Banks had created the character, an unknown issuer had launched the Solana token, and traders wanted Banks to step into the story. The creator's attention could become part of the token's promotion.

Banks said the character was copyrighted and that he planned takedowns for unauthorized merchandise and shitcoins. Brand accounts using the character as a trend were different; he mainly wanted credit. [CoinDesk reported the exchange.](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2024/11/21/chillguy-creator-threatens-legal-action-as-crypto-trenches-scour-tiktok)

Banks had created the character; the token had its own issuer, holders and fee route. The people creating its culture, holding its supply and receiving its payments could be different people. The word **community** can make those roles sound like one group.

## The meme travels farther than the token.

A meme can be copied, remixed and carried into new audiences. A Bigfoot joke can circulate across X without anyone needing permission from a token holder. The token built around a meme has a finite supply, a launch history and a holder distribution.

That difference becomes important when price turns into content. A holder posts a rising chart; the screenshot reaches a new audience; some viewers buy; their orders create another move worth posting. The chart starts feeding the attention that helped move it.

Across community-driven tokens, trading activity responds to salient price moves, returns spill across the segment, and illiquidity and downside tail risk are elevated. [The research examines these market dynamics.](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0275531926003491)

A holder can make fan art, moderate the chat and own the token. The enthusiasm may be genuine; the post still comes from someone whose balance sheet benefits when new buyers arrive.

## A position changes the meaning of a post.

Bitquery examined 8,212,568 Pump.fun launches and found that the developer's first sale occurred within one minute on 65% of launches. Its wallet labels are inferred from the launch transaction, fee payment and the four transactions immediately after launch, not verified real-world identities; a first sale does not establish a full exit. [The investigation details its method.](https://www.bitquery.io/investigations/pump-fun-snipers-bundles)

The timing matters. By the time a token reaches the public feed, its earliest ownership positions may already be in place. A viral post confirms that attention has arrived; it says nothing about who was positioned first, how much supply they hold or how easily they can sell.

Market cap multiplies the quoted price by token supply. It is a valuation snapshot, not the money that entered the token or the liquidity available to exit at that quote.

Wanting a higher price is ordinary self-interest. Opportunism is different: it appears when someone hides a position, presents a conflicted pitch as independent judgment, or withholds information that would change how a buyer reads the promotion. A disclosed holding is a conflict to price in, not proof of dishonesty. A claim of neutrality from an undisclosed holder is a different matter.

## Follow the work, the supply and the fees.

Start with the work that continues when the chart quiets: original posts, art, translations, spaces, moderation, events and jokes. Then inspect the supply: who received tokens, when they received them and how concentrated the holdings remain. Finally trace the money: creator fees, holder rewards and the wallets receiving them.

The roles can overlap. An artist can hold tokens; an early buyer can become a real organizer; a moderator can receive rewards. The holder distribution and fee route show how the token actually allocates ownership and payments.

Pump.fun provides one example of how the cash-flow side can work. Its documentation allows a creator to direct the Creator fee to eligible holders instead of receiving it personally. Holder Rewards have eligibility requirements and distribution costs, and payments are not guaranteed. The platform's setting does not automatically create a royalty for the person who made the original meme. [Pump.fun's fee documentation.](https://pump.fun/docs/fees)

Online communities can also produce value beyond trading. Dogecoin supporters raised more than $30,000 for Jamaica's two-man bobsled team to travel to the 2014 Sochi Winter Olympics. That fundraiser showed real coordination; it did not determine how a separate token's supply or fees were distributed. [PBS covered the fundraiser.](https://www.pbs.org/newshour/world/jamaican-bobsled-team-raises-30000-in-dogecoin-for-trip-to-sochi)

A busy timeline is evidence of activity. It is not proof of broad ownership, independent demand or shared upside. Read the work to understand the culture, the holder distribution to understand ownership, and the fee route to understand who gets paid.

A crowded feed tells you the joke has travelled. The holder map and fee route tell you where the upside went.
