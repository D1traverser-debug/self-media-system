# Visual Prompt Pack v3 --- When Everyone Has a Bag, Who Do You Believe?

## Locked editorial brief

**Title:** When Everyone Has a Bag, Who Do You Believe?\
**Chinese title:** 当所有人都有仓位，你还能相信谁？\
**Subtitle:** Everyone can make a meme famous. Not everyone gets paid
when its token runs.

**Thesis:** Memecoins turn cultural attention into a market signal, then
let the price generated by that attention become fresh evidence that the
signal is real. The market blurs three separate ledgers: cultural
contribution, token ownership and cash-flow rights.

**Author's position:** A fairer memecoin market does not require equal
outcomes. It requires visible claims: who creates the culture, who owns
the supply, and who receives the fees. The next durable meme needs both
a cultural engine that survives a flat chart and an economic engine
whose ownership and fee flows are legible.

## Shared art direction

Premium editorial illustration for an intellectually sharp crypto
publication. Human, contemporary and observant, with a little
tension---not glossy crypto advertising. Use graphite, warm ivory,
cobalt blue and signal orange, with restrained acid-lime accents.
Tactile print grain, layered depth, controlled reflections and clear
negative space. One consistent original abstract meme glyph appears
across the set. It must be generic and not resemble an existing
character, brand or token.

## 01. Cover --- one meme, three claims

**Canvas:** 5:2 horizontal, 2000 × 800 px.\
**Placement:** X Articles header image.\
**Caption:** "Everyone can make a meme famous. Not everyone gets paid
when its token runs."\
**Alt text:** One shared meme symbol moves through creators, online
communities and token holders, while the people creating culture, owning
supply and receiving fees are visibly different.

**Standalone prompt:**

Create a premium editorial cover illustration in a strict 5:2 horizontal
composition for an X Article titled "When Everyone Has a Bag, Who Do You
Believe?"

The visual argument is that one meme creates three different claims:
cultural contribution, token ownership and financial reward. Show one
original abstract meme glyph moving through a connected online crowd. On
the left, an independent artist sketches it and other users remix,
caption and share it. In the center, the same symbol spreads across
phones and social feeds, creating a sense of genuine shared culture. On
the right, a smaller group watches a token-market display and a separate
fee-flow destination, suggesting that ownership and cash receipts may
sit with different people from those doing the creative work.

Some individuals may appear in more than one role. Avoid a moral split
between "good creators" and "bad traders." The crowd should feel real;
the economic claims should feel uneven and visible.

Use graphite, warm ivory, cobalt blue and signal orange, with restrained
acid-lime accents. Editorial collage, tactile paper grain, natural
cinematic light and sophisticated negative space. Keep the key visual
within the central 80% to survive responsive cropping. Leave a clean
upper-left or upper-right area for title typesetting.

No existing meme characters, logos, text, letters, numbers, tickers,
wallet addresses, watermarks, coin piles, rockets, moon imagery, casino
motifs or giant green arrows.

## 02. Inline visual --- the chart advertises itself

**Canvas:** 16:9 horizontal, 1600 × 900 px.\
**Placement:** After Section 02.\
**Caption:** "The chart stops being a record of attention and becomes
part of the next attention cycle."\
**Alt text:** A meme post attracts buyers; a rising chart becomes a
social screenshot; the screenshot draws more viewers back to the meme.

**Standalone prompt:**

Create a clear editorial illustration in 16:9 landscape format showing
the feedback loop between memecoin attention and price.

Build one continuous visual sequence, not a four-panel infographic: an
original meme post catches attention; buyers enter a token market; a
rising chart is captured and reposted; the screenshot brings new viewers
back to the meme. Use repeated visual motifs and subtle directional flow
so the loop is immediately legible without labels or arrows.

Show that the loop can weaken: include a quiet feed or a stalled chart
at the edge of the composition. Do not suggest every rally is
manufactured or every post is paid promotion. The point is that price
and attention can reinforce one another, even when participants are
sincere.

Use the shared graphite, warm ivory, cobalt blue and signal-orange
palette, tactile print grain and restrained reflections. Generic
interface shapes only; no actual exchange UI.

No readable text, numbers, tickers, logos, watermarks, rockets, moons,
casino motifs or giant green arrows.

## 03. Inline visual --- early to the meme, late to the supply

**Canvas:** 16:9 horizontal, 1600 × 900 px.\
**Placement:** After Section 03.\
**Caption:** "The timeline is public. The earliest positions may already
be established."\
**Alt text:** A newcomer discovers a trending meme while a separate,
earlier layer of token ownership has already formed.

**Standalone prompt:**

Create a premium editorial illustration in 16:9 landscape format about
the difference between discovering a meme early and entering a token
early.

Use a layered depth composition. In the foreground, show a newcomer
discovering an original abstract meme glyph on a social feed. Behind and
beneath that visible layer, reveal an earlier launch sequence: a compact
first-trading-block motif, a few wallet-like silhouettes already holding
positions, and a timeline that began before the newcomer saw the post.

The early participants should not look like cartoon villains. This is
about timing and unequal access, not proof of fraud. The image should
make one point clear: public attention often arrives after the token's
first ownership positions have begun to form.

Use graphite, warm ivory, cobalt blue and signal orange, with tactile
print grain and controlled editorial lighting. Leave a clean lower strip
for a manually typeset data card.

No readable text, numbers, wallet addresses, logos, tickers, exchange
UI, watermarks, recognizable public figures, rockets or giant price
arrows.

## 04. Inline visual --- three ledgers, one word

**Canvas:** 16:9 horizontal, 1600 × 900 px.\
**Placement:** After Section 05.\
**Caption:** "Culture, ownership and cash flow are three different
ledgers."\
**Alt text:** Three connected but distinct groups represent cultural
contributors, token owners and fee recipients.

**Standalone prompt:**

Create a clean, sophisticated editorial illustration in 16:9 landscape
format. The concept is the three ledgers hidden inside the word
"community."

Show three connected but distinct visual zones: 1. Cultural
contribution: an original artist, remixers, translators, event
organizers and moderators working with one shared abstract meme glyph.
2. Token ownership: a different group of people holding differently
sized abstract token claims, with a visible sense of uneven allocation.
3. Cash-flow rights: separate destinations receiving creator fees or
holder rewards through a simple, legible flow.

Some people may overlap between zones, but do not collapse all roles
into one generic crowd. Make the distinction immediately visible: the
people creating cultural recognition, owning token supply and receiving
fees are not necessarily the same people.

Avoid moral judgment. Early buyers may have taken risk; creators and
community builders may have contributed labor; fee recipients may be
defined by the token's configuration. The visual should clarify the
different claims, not declare one group good and another bad.

Use the shared palette and editorial style. Leave clean negative space
for three labels to be added manually.

No text, numbers, percentages, logos, tickers, watermarks, wallet
addresses, coin piles, recognizable existing meme characters or generic
neon Web3 backgrounds.
