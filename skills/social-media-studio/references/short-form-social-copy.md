# Short-form Social Copy

> Status: CANDIDATE_REQUIRES_REVALIDATION. The exact owner brief and source material remain authoritative.

## Scope

Standalone short posts, captions, short descriptions and compact social text. This is not a default news script, a video-production pipeline or a long-form article template.

## Method

### 1. Read the actual source

For a video, inspect the full available video and audio before writing. Record observable subject, action, turn, distinctive detail and ending. Separate observation from interpretation. Mark whether the material is factual, fictional, gameplay, synthetic, staged or unknown when that distinction matters.

Do not invent a creator's motive, a character's inner life, a viewer's personal situation or a dramatic backstory. Playful framing is allowed when it is clearly creative and does not masquerade as fact.

### 2. Find the content-specific reason to care

Do not stop at what happens in the clip. Identify what makes this particular moment worth attention: an unexpected action, recognizable situation, unusual contrast, payoff, useful insight, expressive detail or another source-grounded feature.

Do not turn a game, pet or entertainment clip into a news report or generic philosophical monologue unless that is explicitly the requested editorial form.

### 3. Establish audience value

Use the Audience Response reference when resonance, recognition, humor, debate, companionship, sharing, comments, saves or follower growth materially affect the brief. Identify the audience by relevant content preference, not invented demographics. Keep the audience segment and response mechanism grounded in the source or observed audience evidence.

Do not force a fixed emotional category. If the strongest reason to care is simply that the moment is funny, let it be funny.

### 4. Select the angle before polishing the sentence

Compare materially different angles when the creative direction is uncertain. Reject generic plot recap, empty hype, invented emotional premise or distant narrator voice. If the core angle is weak, reopen angle selection; do not decorate a rejected concept with better adjectives.

### 5. Give every requested field a distinct job

- **Standalone post/caption:** one clear thought, joke, observation or value.
- **Title:** a truthful reason to open.
- **Description:** context, voice or an additional layer the title does not already perform.
- **Cover copy:** a compact visual hook with its own job; do not merely repeat the title.
- **CTA:** optional, specific and natural. Do not ask for likes, shares, comments and follows by default.
- **Hashtags:** relevant discovery context only; no padding or invented trend claims.

### 6. Match voice and audience distance

Use the requested language, platform voice, audience and tone. Familiarity is not permission to pretend to know the viewer's private life. Avoid canned slang, fake anecdotes, forced rhetorical questions, generic humanizer tricks and formulaic opening phrases.

When multiple locales are requested, preserve the underlying content promise while localizing idiom, rhythm and cultural references. Do not translate word for word or invent separate channel positioning unless requested.

### 7. Cold-read the result

Check whether the first line earns attention without lying; the text offers concrete audience value; the speaker-audience relationship fits; interpretations are grounded; title, description, cover copy and CTA do different jobs; and there is no filler, news narration, generic philosophy, forced intimacy or engagement bait.

The quality-gates reference remains the release checklist. Structural PASS does not certify creative quality or predict performance.

## Re-entry

If the owner rejects the angle, reopen the angle decision and challenge the original premise. Do not keep revising wording on top of a rejected concept.
