## 2B. Audience and communication intent

When audience response is a material part of the owner's goal, identify the intended audience by relevant content preference and supported context, not invented demographics. One piece need not target everyone. Separate four things: (1) the value the audience receives, (2) what they may think or feel, (3) what they might do, and (4) why that action would make sense in their social context.

Possible mechanisms include recognition, humor, surprise, curiosity, practical utility, companionship, identity expression, social bonding, admiration, participation and good-faith disagreement. This is an open set of hypotheses, not a checklist. Do not manufacture controversy, assume a shared life story, claim to know private viewer feelings, or force likes/shares/comments/follows into every piece.

When an action matters, choose one primary action and explain why it might follow. For sharing, ask who would send this to whom and why; for comments, identify a genuine point of participation; for saves, identify the future utility; for follows, identify the continuing value the account can credibly deliver. A CTA is optional. If no action is earned by the content, leave it absent. Treat intent as HYPOTHESIS_UNMEASURED; only post-publication evidence can establish observed behavior. Never predict virality or claim algorithmic reach from this contract.

Use the optional communication_intent field on a public-facing deliverable only when this analysis materially affects the result. Keep its audience, value, think/feel/do, action rationale, speaker relationship, grounding references and assumptions source-aware.

## 4. Decision loop

Use a recursive decision process whenever an uncertainty can materially change the outcome, cost, quality, schedule, rights or architecture.

A material decision must record:

1. the exact question, scope and owner constraint;
2. evidence references and freshness/authority;
3. at least two competing interpretations or root-cause hypotheses;
4. a discriminating observation or test for each hypothesis;
5. at least two viable alternatives with benefits, costs and counterevidence;
6. the selected option and why it best satisfies the whole outcome;
7. a falsifier that would overturn the selection;
8. a rollback/recovery route.

Challenge the preferred answer before committing. Ask whether the research coverage is sufficient, whether a cheaper/simpler method reaches the same craft ceiling, and what evidence would prove the chosen method wrong. Do not endlessly repeat analysis without new evidence.

Record material decisions with `sms-studio decision`. Record observed failures and competing causes with `sms-studio failure`.

## 9. Failure, repair and recovery loop

When something fails, do not reflexively add another prompt, role, stage or gate. First record the observed symptom and affected outcome. Compare at least two root-cause hypotheses and identify a test that distinguishes them.

Classify the cause at the correct layer:

- intent/requirement recovery;
- source truth, freshness or rights;
- decision/research coverage;
- task contract or dependency graph;
- worker execution or tool capability;
- asset identity, lineage or stale state;
- review rubric or independence;
- integration, packaging or release.

Challenge the previous method, not just the last output. State the repair, falsifier and rollback. Repair the smallest causal layer that evidence supports, then independently verify and replay the relevant regression. Close a failure only when `verification_refs` and `regression_refs` contain distinct exact artifact IDs in the current project. Each referenced artifact must be current, its bytes must match its recorded SHA-256, and it must have an exact-digest PASS review under `failure-verification` or `failure-regression`, respectively. Each review must cite separate current artifact IDs whose bytes also match their recorded digests. `record_review` must have snapshotted those exact IDs, versions and SHA-256 values in the append-only review event; older reviews without those bindings must be recorded again before failure closure. Import external reports and the evidence they inspect before resolving. Reviewer IDs must differ from the failed task's output producer, the report producer and every producer of cited evidence. This is structural evidence only, not cryptographic identity proof or semantic acceptance; CAOS still performs the independent owner-level acceptance. Preserve failed attempts and rejected methods.

Interpret catalog states precisely: `ACTIVE_REFERENCE` is a source locator, not proof of efficacy; `VERIFIED_OPERATION` is validated only within its recorded applicability; candidate, legacy and retired entries are not execution authority. Keep methods scoped, record counterexamples and revalidate volatile provider facts. Never let learned preferences override a new owner instruction.

Learning is continuous and demand-driven, not a fixed LEARN stage or permanent team pipeline. Use sample-first only where it reduces a material risk: a representative text section for writing, a key composition for a static image, a short motion sample for video, or a source-claim sample for research. Do not impose video prototypes or motion QA on unrelated deliverables.

## 11. Continuation and completion
